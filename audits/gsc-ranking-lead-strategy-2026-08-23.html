Diagnosis

Three things are true, and only one of them is obvious

The 0.38% CTR looks like a title-tag problem. It is not — your titles are well built, fee-led, and render correctly. The CTR is low because of what is being counted and where your pages sit.

Finding 01 · Measurement

Half your impressions are a mirage, and they are hiding the real numbers

The CGPA calculator cluster pulls 1,403,518 impressions — 51% of everything — at 0.25% CTR, and has produced zero leads in six months. Queries like 7.5 cgpa in percentage (27,166 impressions, position 4.55, 0.16% CTR) are answered by Google's own inline calculator. You rank, the user never clicks, and if they did they would not be a lead.

Strip the calculator out and your real site looks completely different: 7,033 clicks on 1,341,223 impressions = 0.52% CTR, converting at 4.9% to leads. That is your actual business. Every decision below is made on that number, not the headline one.
Do this

Leave the calculator live — it costs nothing and builds topical authority. But add a GSC regex filter excluding cgpa|gpa|percentage and make that your reporting view. Stop optimising against a number that 51% junk traffic controls.

Finding 02 · Structural — this is the big one

Your review blog and your programme hub are fighting each other for the same query

For every major university you have published two pages chasing [university] online mba fees: a blog review and a /universities/…/mba hub. Google cannot decide, so it half-ranks both. The blog wins position 7–9. The hub — the page carrying the fee table, the specialisation list and the schema — gets buried at 10 to 40.

Finding 03 · Coverage

People are asking you about universities you have never written about

All 128 universities exist in lib/data.ts, so raw coverage is not the gap. The gap is review content. Cross-referencing your 346 leads against Search Console shows universities generating real enquiries with almost no search visibility — which means those leads are arriving despite you, not because of you.

The clearest case is Mody University: 7 leads from 9 total impressions. That is demand with effectively zero organic presence. Same shape at JSS AHER (4 leads, 173 impressions) and BIT Mesra (3 leads, 288 impressions).

Rollout · Run in this order

Four phases, twelve weeks

The order matters. Consolidation must land before new content, or the new pages inherit the same split-signal problem.

01Wk 1–2

Stop the bleeding Merge

Take the top 5 clusters — Galgotias, Amity, BITS Pilani, Symbiosis, Jain. For each: move the hub's fee table, specialisation list and Course schema into the blog post body, then 301 the hub to the blog.

  • Add redirects in next.config.js; the middleware allowlist and resolveSpec canonical logic you fixed in the last five commits already give you the pattern.
  • Keep the hub's Offer/fee schema fragment — port it, do not drop it. That schema is why some hubs pick up rich results.
  • Point every internal link that targeted the hub at the blog instead. Zero orphan links, per house rules.
  • Deep-buried spec pages at position 27–50 (jain…/mba/international-finance-acca at 32.98, chandigarh…/mba at 40.05) — noindex them or fold into the parent. 17 pages, 7,685 impressions, 31 clicks. They are dilution, not assets.
02Wk 3–5

Finish the merge, then push for position 3 Merge

Run the same operation across the remaining 12 clusters. Then work the surviving URLs upward — this is where the fee-query rankings actually move.

  • Rewrite each merged page's H2 set to match the query shape exactly: fees, total fees, fee structure, placement, is it valid. galgotias university mba total fees and galgotia mba fees structure are separate ranking queries you are already half-catching.
  • Add an FAQ block answering the fee question in the first 40 words — that is what wins the snippet at position 3.
  • Internal links: 5–10 per post, from the high-authority pages. /blog/iim-ranking-india-2026-all-iims-list (621 clicks) and /blog/is-manipal-university-jaipur-fake-or-legit-2026 (336 clicks) are your two strongest link sources. Use them.
03Wk 6–9

Build the seven proven-demand reviews Build

Parul, Bharati Vidyapeeth, Bangalore University, DDU Gorakhpur, Amrita (MBA), SPPU, Mody. Use /university-review so house style and the fee-verification directive are enforced automatically.

  • Publish the blog first, and link the existing hub to it rather than creating a competing hub — do not recreate the problem you just fixed.
  • Cross-check every NAAC, NIRF, AICTE claim against Supabase before publishing. NIRF ranks must state the category.
  • Mody is the highest-leverage of the seven: 7 leads on 9 impressions means the demand is real and the SERP is wide open.
04Wk 10–12

Open the BCA and BBA front Build

62 of your leads are BBA or BCA and you have four review posts between them. The hubs already rank at 8–10 with 2,000–4,000 impressions each, which means the pages are acceptable and only the content depth is missing.

  • Start with the hubs already pulling volume: Galgotias, GLA, Chitkara, Integral, Jamia Hamdard, DY Patil, SGT, Guru Ghasidas.
  • Target the fee-query variants directly — parul university bca fees, chandigarh university bca total fees, integral university bca fees per semester, sgt university bca fees, gla bca fees — all sitting at position 9–11 with near-zero CTR.
  • Switch your GSC reporting to the CGPA-excluded view before measuring any of this. Otherwise the calculator noise will mask a genuine doubling.
